# Surrogate-Based Bayesian Inference: Uncertainty Quantification and Active Learning

- **arXiv**: [2603.13646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13646)
- **Authors**: Andrew Gerard Roberts, Michael C. Dietze, Jonathan H. Huggins
- **Submitted**: 2026-03-13
- **Affiliation**: Boston University (Computing & Data Sciences, Earth & Environment, Mathematics & Statistics)
- **Keywords**: active learning, surrogate modeling

## Methods

This is a comprehensive review paper that synthesizes the literature on surrogate-based Bayesian inference into a coherent framework. The paper identifies and organizes key methodological components:

1. **Surrogate construction**: Reviews emulator architectures for replacing expensive forward models, including Gaussian Processes (GPs), polynomial chaos expansions (PCE), neural network emulators, and deep operator networks. Evaluates trade-offs in expressiveness, uncertainty calibration, and computational cost.

2. **Surrogate uncertainty propagation**: A central contribution is the systematic treatment of how surrogate approximation error propagates into posterior uncertainty. The paper categorizes approaches:
   - **Plug-in methods**: Replace the true likelihood with the surrogate likelihood, ignoring surrogate uncertainty (fast but biased).
   - **Marginalization methods**: Integrate over surrogate uncertainty by treating the emulator as a random function, yielding wider but better-calibrated posteriors.
   - **Correction methods**: Post-hoc adjustments to plug-in posteriors using importance weighting or calibration.

3. **Active learning for surrogate refinement**: Sequential experimental design strategies that iteratively refine the surrogate in regions most relevant to the posterior. Acquisition functions reviewed include:
   - Integrated variance reduction (IVAR)
   - Expected information gain (EIG)
   - Posterior-weighted criteria that focus computational budget on high-posterior-density regions
   - Bayesian optimization-inspired strategies adapted for inference

4. **Workflow integration**: The paper proposes a unified workflow: (a) initial space-filling design, (b) surrogate fitting with uncertainty, (c) active refinement targeting posterior-relevant regions, (d) posterior inference with surrogate uncertainty propagation, (e) convergence diagnostics.

## Conclusion

The review identifies several open challenges: (1) principled methods for propagating surrogate uncertainty remain underused in practice despite theoretical advantages, (2) active learning criteria are often developed independently of the inference goal, leading to suboptimal designs, (3) convergence diagnostics for surrogate-based posteriors are underdeveloped. The paper argues that tighter integration of surrogate uncertainty propagation and active learning within Bayesian workflows can yield more efficient and reliable inference, particularly for computationally expensive models in ecology, climate science, and engineering.
