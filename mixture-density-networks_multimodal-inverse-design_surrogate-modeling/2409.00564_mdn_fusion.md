# Using Deep Learning to Design High Aspect Ratio Fusion Devices

- **arXiv**: [2409.00564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00564)
- **Authors**: P. Curvo, D. R. Ferreira, R. Jorge
- **Submitted**: 2024-08-31
- **Keywords**: Mixture Density Networks, multimodal inverse design, surrogate modeling

## Methods

1. **Mixture Density Network (MDN)**: The inverse model outputs a Gaussian mixture distribution over design parameters (stellarator configurations) for a given target property set. Each mixture component represents a distinct mode of the non-unique inverse mapping.

2. **High aspect ratio parameterisation**: Stellarator geometry is reduced to a compact set of parameters capturing the essential shape degrees of freedom, making the inverse problem tractable for neural network learning.

3. **Forward surrogate training**: A neural network surrogate is pre-trained on simulation data to map design parameters to physics objectives (rotational transform, elongation, plasma beta, fast particle confinement).

4. **Probabilistic output generation**: At inference time, the MDN produces multiple valid stellarator configurations with associated probabilities, directly addressing the one-to-many nature of the inverse design problem.

5. **Physics-constrained evaluation**: Generated configurations are validated against physics-based criteria to ensure they satisfy confinement quality requirements.

## Conclusion

The MDN-based inverse design approach reliably generates high aspect ratio fusion device configurations with favourable confinement properties, directly handling the inherent non-uniqueness of the inverse mapping through probabilistic multi-modal outputs. The surrogate-based pipeline reduces computational cost by orders of magnitude compared to traditional simulation-based optimisation of stellarator geometries.
