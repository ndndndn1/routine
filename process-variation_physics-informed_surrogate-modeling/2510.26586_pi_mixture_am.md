# Physics-Informed Mixture Models and Surrogate Models for Precision Additive Manufacturing

- **arXiv**: [2510.26586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26586)
- **Authors**: Sebastian Basterrech et al.
- **Submitted**: 2025-10-30 (revised 2025-11-09)
- **Keywords**: process variation, physics-informed, surrogate modeling

## Methods

This paper addresses process variation in laser-based additive manufacturing (AM) by combining physics-informed mixture models with surrogate models for defect identification.

1. **Physics-informed Gaussian Mixture Model (PI-GMM)**: A GMM is extended with physics-based constraints derived from the thermal and melt-pool dynamics of laser-metal interactions. The mixture components correspond to distinct process regimes (e.g., conduction mode, keyhole mode, balling). Physics priors constrain the component means and covariances to align with known thermodynamic relationships between process parameters (laser power, scan speed, layer thickness) and defect types.

2. **Surrogate model for process-structure linkage**: A neural network surrogate maps process parameters to predicted melt-pool characteristics (width, depth, temperature). This surrogate is trained on simulation data from thermal finite element models and replaces expensive physics simulations during inference.

3. **Sensitivity to process variations**: The physics-informed formulation ensures that the model is sensitive to meaningful physical parameter variations — small changes in laser power or scan speed that cross regime boundaries are correctly detected, while noise in non-physical dimensions is suppressed.

4. **Defect classification pipeline**: The PI-GMM clusters process parameter configurations into defect regimes (Lack of Fusion, Keyhole porosity, Balling, acceptable). The surrogate provides fast prediction of melt-pool features that inform the mixture assignment.

5. **Two AM processes evaluated**: Directed Energy Deposition (DED) and Laser Powder Bed Fusion (LPBF) with real-world experimental datasets.

## Conclusion

The physics-informed mixture model outperforms purely data-driven clustering (standard GMM, k-means) in identifying defect regimes, particularly near regime boundaries where process variation has the greatest impact. The physics constraints reduce the data requirement compared to large deep learning models and improve generalization to unseen process parameter combinations. The surrogate model provides real-time inference capability while maintaining physical plausibility. The approach is demonstrated to be effective for both DED and LPBF processes, suggesting generalizability across AM modalities. The framework offers enhanced interpretability — each mixture component has a clear physical meaning — while maintaining parsimony.
