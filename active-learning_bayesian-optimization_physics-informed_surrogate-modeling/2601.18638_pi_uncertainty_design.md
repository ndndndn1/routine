# Physics-Informed Uncertainty Enables Reliable AI-driven Design

- **arXiv**: [2601.18638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18638)
- **Authors**: Tingkai Xue, Chin Chun Ooi, Yang Jiang, Luu Trung Pham Duong, Pao-Hsiung Chiu, Weijiang Zhao, Nagarajan Raghavan, My Ha Dao
- **Submitted**: 2026-01-26
- **Keywords**: physics-informed, surrogate modeling, bayesian optimization, active learning

## Methods

1. **Physics-Informed Uncertainty (PIU)**: The degree to which a surrogate model's prediction violates fundamental physical laws (e.g., energy conservation, electromagnetic reciprocity) is used as a computationally cheap proxy for predictive uncertainty—no ensemble or MC dropout needed.

2. **Deep learning surrogate**: A neural network maps design parameters of frequency-selective surfaces (FSS) in the 20–30 GHz band to transmission/reflection coefficients (real and imaginary). The surrogate replaces expensive full-wave EM solvers.

3. **Multi-fidelity uncertainty-aware optimisation**: An iterative loop balances exploration (high-PIU regions where physical violations are large) and exploitation (promising designs), akin to Bayesian optimisation with PIU as the uncertainty signal.

4. **Active sampling**: New simulation points are selected where PIU is highest, refining the surrogate in data-sparse but physically important regions.

5. **Comparison**: Benchmarked against ensemble-based uncertainty baselines; PIU achieves comparable discrimination of good vs. bad predictions at a fraction of the cost.

## Conclusion

Integrating physics-informed uncertainty into the surrogate-assisted optimisation loop raises inverse design success rates from < 10 % to > 50 % while reducing computational cost by an order of magnitude compared to using a high-fidelity solver alone. PIU is a fundamentally different paradigm from data-driven uncertainty estimation, requiring no additional model copies or sampling, and is applicable wherever physical constraints can be formulated analytically.
