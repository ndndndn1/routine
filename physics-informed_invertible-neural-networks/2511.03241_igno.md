# A Unified Physics-Informed Generative Operator Framework for General Inverse Problems (IGNO)

- **arXiv**: [2511.03241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03241)
- **Authors**: Junkai Bian, Ting Wei, Yizheng Wang, Ning Lin
- **Submitted**: 2025-11-05
- **Keywords**: physics-informed, Invertible Neural Networks (normalizing flow)

## Methods

IGNO (Invertible Generative Neural Operator) is a physics-informed framework that solves PDE-governed inverse problems without labeled training pairs, unifying point-measurement and operator-valued data.

1. **Latent space encoding**: High-dimensional coefficient fields (potentially discontinuous) are encoded into a low-dimensional latent space z via a learned encoder. Neural operator decoders reconstruct both the PDE coefficients and the corresponding PDE solutions from z.

2. **Physics-informed training**: Training uses only physics constraints — PDE residuals computed from the decoded coefficient-solution pairs serve as the loss function. No labeled (input, output) pairs are needed, making the approach applicable to problems where ground-truth inverse solutions are unavailable.

3. **Normalizing flow prior**: A normalizing flow f_phi maps the latent space to a standard Gaussian distribution N(0, I). This provides:
   - Statistically informed initializations for the inversion optimization
   - Regularization of the latent space, preventing mode collapse
   - Acceleration of convergence in noisy or ill-posed settings

4. **Gradient-based inversion**: Given measurements, inversion is performed by optimizing in the latent space:

   z* = argmin_z || M(D_theta(z)) - y_obs ||^2 + lambda * log p_Z(z)

   where D_theta is the decoder, M is the measurement operator, and log p_Z provides the normalizing flow regularization. The low dimensionality of z makes this optimization efficient.

5. **Neural operator architecture**: The decoder uses a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) backbone, enabling mesh-independent evaluation and strong generalization across discretizations.

## Conclusion

IGNO achieves reconstruction errors 3-6x lower than state-of-the-art methods across diverse inverse problems, including recovery of discontinuous coefficients from solution-based measurements, Electrical Impedance Tomography (EIT) with operator-based measurements, and multi-scale problems. The normalizing flow prior is critical: it stabilizes optimization under severe noise and provides physically meaningful initializations. The framework demonstrates strong extrapolation to out-of-distribution targets and robustness to varying noise levels, making it practical for real-world scientific applications where labeled data is scarce but physics models are available.
