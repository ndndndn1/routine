# Data Curation for Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials by Determinantal Point Processes

- **arXiv**: [2603.22160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22160)
- **Authors**: Joanna Zou, Youssef Marzouk
- **Submitted**: 2026-03
- **Affiliation**: MIT, Computational Science & Engineering
- **Keywords**: Determinantal Point Processes, active learning

## Methods

This paper applies Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) to the task of curating diverse and informative training sets for Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials (MLIPs).

1. **Problem setup**: Training MLIPs requires labeling atomic configurations with energies and forces from expensive quantum mechanical (QM) calculations (e.g., DFT). The challenge is to select a compact but maximally informative subset of configurations from a large pool of unlabeled candidates.

2. **DPP-based subset selection**: A DPP is defined over the pool of candidate configurations using a kernel constructed from molecular descriptors (e.g., SOAP, ACE). The DPP assigns higher probability to subsets that are diverse in descriptor space:

   P(S) proportional to det(L_S)

   where L_S is the kernel submatrix indexed by subset S. The repulsive nature of DPPs ensures selected configurations cover distinct regions of the conformational landscape.

3. **Kernel construction**: The similarity kernel k(x_i, x_j) is built from molecular descriptors that encode local atomic environments. The kernel captures both structural similarity and energetic relevance, ensuring diversity in physically meaningful dimensions rather than arbitrary coordinate space.

4. **Comparison with active learning baselines**: The DPP approach is compared against:
   - Random sampling
   - Farthest-point sampling (FPS) in descriptor space
   - Uncertainty-based active learning (query-by-committee with ensemble MLIPs)
   - CUR decomposition

5. **Evaluation on hafnium oxide**: The selected training subsets are used to train MLIPs, and prediction accuracy on held-out test configurations is measured for both energies and forces.

## Conclusion

DPPs are competitive with established methods (FPS, active learning) for constructing compact training sets, while offering several advantages: (1) they provide a principled probabilistic framework (rather than greedy heuristics), (2) they naturally balance quality and diversity through the kernel, and (3) they can be applied in an unsupervised manner without requiring an initial trained model (unlike uncertainty-based active learning). The hafnium oxide experiments show improved accuracy and robustness compared to random sampling, with comparable or better performance versus FPS. Future directions include extending DPPs to online/active learning schemes for iterative data augmentation during molecular dynamics simulations and handling heterogeneous or multimodal data.
