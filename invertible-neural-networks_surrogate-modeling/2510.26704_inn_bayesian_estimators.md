# How Regularization Terms Make Invertible Neural Networks Bayesian Point Estimators

- **arXiv**: [2510.26704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26704)
- **Authors**: Nick Heilenkötter
- **Submitted**: 2025-10 (preprint, submitted to Inverse Problems, IOP Publishing)
- **Keywords**: Invertible Neural Networks, surrogate modeling

## Methods

This paper provides a theoretical analysis connecting regularization strategies for invertible neural networks (INNs) to classical Bayesian point estimators in inverse problems.

1. **INN as forward-inverse pair**: An invertible network G_theta simultaneously approximates the forward operator F (mapping parameters to observations) and, upon inversion, provides a reconstruction map G_theta^{-1} from observations back to parameters. The invertibility constraint ensures stability and interpretability.

2. **Two regularization terms**:
   - **Regularizer R1 (posterior mean connection)**: Derived from enforcing the inverted network to approximate the conditional expectation E[x|y]. Training with R1 yields reconstructions that approximate the posterior mean, which is the Bayes-optimal estimator under squared-error loss.
   - **Regularizer R2 (MAP connection)**: Encourages the inverse map to approximate the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate. This is achieved by incorporating a data-dependent prior that penalizes deviations from the learned forward model, connecting to the MAP estimator under Gaussian noise assumptions.

3. **Surrogate integration**: For PDE-based inverse problems where the true forward operator is expensive, a learned neural surrogate replaces the physics simulator during training. The regularization framework remains valid when the surrogate is sufficiently accurate, establishing a bridge between surrogate-based inference and INN-based reconstruction.

4. **Bayesian uncertainty**: While INNs provide point estimates, the latent space of the INN can be used to sample from an approximate posterior by injecting noise into the latent variables and inverting, providing uncertainty quantification.

## Conclusion

The theoretical analysis reveals that common regularization practices in INN training implicitly recover Bayesian point estimators. R1-regularized INNs converge to the posterior mean (MMSE), while R2-regularized INNs converge to the MAP estimate. This connection is validated on elliptic PDE inverse problems using learned surrogate forward models. The work provides principled guidance for choosing regularization in INN-based inverse solvers: R1 for minimum variance reconstruction, R2 for mode-seeking behavior. The surrogate-INN pipeline achieves computational speedups of several orders of magnitude over MCMC while maintaining calibrated uncertainty estimates.
