# Active Learning of Model Discrepancy with Bayesian Experimental Design

- **arXiv**: [2502.05372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05372)
- **Authors**: Huchen Yang, Chuanqi Chen, Jin-Long Wu
- **Submitted**: 2025-02-07 (published in Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, Vol. 446, 2025)
- **Keywords**: active learning, bayesian adaptive design, sequential design of experiments, surrogate modeling

## Methods

1. **Sequential Bayesian Experimental Design (BED)**: An iterative loop selects the most informative experimental conditions to maximise expected information gain about model parameters and model-form errors.

2. **Ensemble Kalman Inversion (EKI)**: Used as a computationally tractable proxy for Bayesian inference within the BED loop, avoiding expensive MCMC sampling at each iteration.

3. **Ensemble-based information gain indicator**: Approximates the expected information gain under Gaussian assumptions using initial and updated EKI ensemble statistics, providing a cheap scoring function for candidate experiments.

4. **Neural-network correction term**: A data-driven model learns the discrepancy between a physics-based (imperfect) model and reality. This correction is updated online as new data arrives from each BED iteration.

5. **Hybrid online learning + BED**: The discrepancy model and experimental design co-evolve—correcting model errors prevents the BED loop from selecting misleading experiments.

6. **Validation**: Demonstrated on digital twin scenarios in manufacturing and autonomous systems engineering.

## Conclusion

Iteratively learning model discrepancy within a sequential BED loop prevents the experimental design from being misled by uncorrected model errors, resulting in more informative data collection. The ensemble-based indicator is computationally efficient compared to MCMC while accurately assessing data informativeness. The framework is validated on practical engineering digital twin applications.
