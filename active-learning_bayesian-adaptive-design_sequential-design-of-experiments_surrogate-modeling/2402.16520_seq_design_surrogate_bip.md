# Sequential Design for Surrogate Modeling in Bayesian Inverse Problems

- **arXiv**: [2402.16520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16520)
- **Authors**: Paul Lartaud, Philippe Humbert, Josselin Garnier
- **Submitted**: 2024-02-26 (revised 2025-01-02, v3; published in Technometrics)
- **Keywords**: active learning, sequential design of experiments, surrogate modeling, bayesian adaptive design

## Methods

1. **Gaussian Process (GP) surrogate**: A GP emulator replaces a costly computer code (forward model) in a Bayesian inverse problem. The posterior distribution over model parameters is approximated via the GP predictive distribution.

2. **Stepwise Uncertainty Reduction (SUR) framework**: Each new design point is chosen by minimising the expectation of an uncertainty metric conditioned on the yet-unknown observation—a principled sequential decision framework.

3. **IP-SUR strategy**: A novel goal-oriented I-optimal criterion that uses a weighted Integrated Mean Square Prediction Error (wIMSPE) as the uncertainty metric. The weights concentrate the reduction effort on the high-posterior-density region relevant to the inverse problem.

4. **Constraint Set Query (CSQ)**: An alternative strategy adapted from D-optimal design, constraining the candidate search to a Mahalanobis-distance ball around the current MAP estimate.

5. **Convergence guarantee**: The paper proves almost-sure convergence of the IP-SUR uncertainty metric—a rare theoretical guarantee in sequential design.

6. **Benchmark comparisons**: Evaluated against naive designs (random, space-filling), classical D-optimal, and Bayes risk minimisation on multiple test cases including scalar and multi-output settings.

## Conclusion

IP-SUR outperforms naive and classical sequential strategies in efficiently reducing posterior uncertainty for Bayesian inverse problems, while enjoying theoretical convergence guarantees. The strategy is tractable for both scalar and multi-output GP surrogates, making it practical for real-world forward models with limited computational budgets.
